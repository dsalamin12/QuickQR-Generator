How to Create a QR Code for a Website or Link

QuickQR Guide  ·  Free  ·  4 min read

A URL QR code is the simplest way to bridge print and digital — one scan and anyone lands straight on your website, landing page, menu, portfolio, or online store. No typing a web address, no searching, no mistyped links.

It's the most flexible QR type there is, because it works for literally any page on the internet.

Who Should Use a URL QR Code?

  • Small business owners — link posters, flyers, and packaging to your website
  • Marketers — send print ad readers to a specific landing page
  • Event organisers — link to registration pages or event details
  • Creators & freelancers — point people to a portfolio or booking page
  • Retailers — add product pages or reviews to packaging

Tips for a Better URL QR Code

  • Link to a specific, relevant page — not just your homepage — so the scan feels worth it
  • Keep the destination page mobile-friendly, since almost everyone scanning is on a phone
  • Test before printing — scan it yourself on two different phones to confirm it opens correctly
  • Add enough contrast between the QR code colour and its background so scanners can read it easily

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I change where the QR code points after I've printed it?

No — QuickQR generates static QR codes, meaning the destination URL is encoded directly into the pattern and can't be edited afterward. If you expect to change the destination link later (for example, a seasonal promotion), consider linking to a page on your own site that you control and can update anytime, rather than a one-off URL.

Does the QR code expire?

No. There's no expiry date — it will keep working for as long as the destination page exists.

Is my link stored anywhere?

No. QuickQR runs entirely in your browser. Your URL is encoded directly into the QR code and is never sent to or stored on any server.
